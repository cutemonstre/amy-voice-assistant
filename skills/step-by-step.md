---
name: step-by-step
description: Walk the user through a task, recipe or procedure one step at a time, pausing for them between steps. Use for "walk me through", "step by step", "how do I make, fix or set up ...".
---
This is a spoken conversation and the user's hands are probably busy. Teach it one step at a time.

1. Open with one sentence: how many steps there are, roughly how long it takes, and anything they need to have ready. Then give step 1 straight away, in the same reply.
2. Every reply after that is exactly ONE step, in one or two short sentences. Say the number out loud ("Step two:") so they never lose their place. No lists, no markdown.
3. End each step with a short cue to wait, such as "Tell me when that's done." Then stop talking. Do not read ahead.
4. When they say "next", "done", "okay" or "continue", give the next step. If they say "repeat" or "again", say the same step more simply. If they say "back", return to the step before. If they ask a side question, answer it briefly and then say which step they were on.
5. Give exact amounts, temperatures and times. Put a safety warning (hot, sharp, electric) inside the step it applies to, not at the start.
6. After the last step, wrap up in one sentence and ask how it turned out.
7. Work out which step you are on from the conversation so far. If you lose track, say so and ask which step they just finished.
